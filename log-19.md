THE FRICTION POINT

The Unfiltered Realist

LOG 018 ended on a question.

Same gate, different arrival.

Does the path to a position change what happens at it?

Two people stand at the same gate.

Same position, same conditions.

One crosses, one does not.

The easy answer is character.

The model’s answer is conditions.

A third answer is history: where they came from, what they tried before, who they know.

We wrote a test for it, before coding a single case.

This log reports what happened when we tried to run it.

The short version:

It could not be run.

THE BREAKDOWN

The Village Analyst

Method note.

The test was written down before any coding, with its rules for what counts as evidence and what counts as a result.

Three rival explanations:

H1. Conditions only. Arrival and history add nothing.

H2. Pathway. Where conditions match, arrival and history go with the outcome.

H3. Disposition. Behaviour we can observe, regularity, learning, time horizon, explains more than either.

And a fourth, stated honestly: the scheme may not work on the data we have.

That outcome has a name, instrument failure, and it counts as a result.

The rule that matters most is the smallest one.

At least eight codable cases, and at least two matched pairs.

Otherwise the result reads: pilot; no inference.

A pair is two people at the same position, with the same conditions, different outcomes, and a difference in history or in how they arrived.

One disclosure.

The test was drafted after reading 16 entries, some of them already carrying an outcome.

So it is not a blind test.

That is why the label was fixed in advance: pilot, whatever it shows.

The screen.

Every entry in the observation record was screened against five tests.

Is it dated?

Can who, where and what be checked by someone else?

Is it about a person or a small actor, not a market?

Is consent recorded?

Does it describe an event at a gate?

Result:

87 entries screened

4  passed

11  observer as subject

 5  identifiable, consent unknown (never used)

43  frameworks and principles, no event

24  group, platform or market level; plans; no consent recorded; tools and lists

Five further copies of entries already counted were not counted twice.

Nineteen of the 87 carry no date.

The four that passed share one shape.

One day in July.

One observer.

One time-point each.

Each records an income source and a friction.

None records what happened next.

Their consent is one line: verbal consent noted.

The outcomes the test needs, crossed, stalled, left, returned, can only be seen later.

Nothing was collected later.

Two entries came close.

A street conversation about paying daily instead of saving.

A reader asking for links to online gigs.

Both failed the same test: no consent recorded.

A good remark is not an observation.

MODEL CHECK

The Good, the Bad, the Ugly

The good

The screen did its job.

It excluded consistently, though by one screener, with no second reader to check.

The rule written in advance meant no argument about what to do with a thin result.

The bad

Interview template captures income source and biggest friction; it does not systematically capture arrival pathway or follow-up outcomes. The crypto line is only a trace of history.

We designed the question after the instrument.

And of 87 entries, 4 could be coded.

About half of what we filed was interpretation.

The ugly

The test could not run.

It needs eight cases. We have four.

We could have coded the four anyway.

Four snapshots produce a table.

A table looks like a result.

We chose not to.

The test could not run on the available data. That is a finding about the evidence and the instrument - not a verdict on the model.

A result from that would have been a story with numbers on it.

What this does not show

Nothing about H1, H2 or H3.

Not supported.

Not contradicted.

This would be wrong if eight entries with a recorded outcome and recorded consent existed.

The four included records say verbal consent noted.

Model v1.1 is unchanged.

Nothing in this log adds a concept.

Trust and Time stay open candidates.




WHAT A REAL TEST NEEDS

Three prompts, added to every field interview, with explicit consent:

1. «How did you come to this?»

No leading.

2. «What happened before? What did you try?»

3. A follow-up at 30 days and at 90 days.

With consent to be contacted again.

And the handling that goes with them:

Consent in writing or on a recording.

A code instead of a name.

Location at county or ward level only.

Age as a range.

No handles or money figures in stored text.

Anonymous by default, not on request.

The target is modest.

Eight cases and two matched pairs are minimum eligibility conditions, not a guarantee that the hypotheses will be distinguishable.

If interviews start this month, the 90-day outcomes arrive in January 2027 at the earliest.

That is the real timeline.

THE PLAYBOOK

The Stoic Survivor

You can run a small version of this yourself.

Pick one person standing at a gate you know.

Ask for consent first.

Say what it is for.

Write a code, not a name.

Keep handles and money figures out of your notes.

Ask the two questions.

How did you come to this?

What happened before?

Write the answers in their words.

Date it.

If they would rather not, stop.

Come back in 30 days, and write what happened.

That is one case.

Do not read a pattern into three.

Count them.

The count is the honest part.

FOR FIRST-TIME READERS

A field journal from rural Kenya.

This log follows the route between earning a digital reward and getting value into a form or destination the participant can use. It records one incomplete transaction trail and a separate, earlier conversion test.

THE FRICTION POINT

The Unfiltered Realist

This is not about big theories.

It is about the distance between - I earned it and I can use it - and the conditions that shape that distance when the steps happen in North Kinangop, Kenya.

An online activity offered a choice: redeem the reward as crypto or take a gift card. The crypto route was chosen.

From that decision onward, the value had to pass through systems with their own rules, thresholds and terms.

Log 019 left one question: when the conversion works, who keeps the value?

This log does not answer it. It examines what the records establish - and where the trail breaks.

THE BREAKDOWN

The Village Analyst

Earn.

An online task platform recorded 2,325.45 points, redeemed on 22 August 2026. At the reported platform rate of 1,000 points to $1, the reward was worth approximately $2.33.

The platform set the redemption terms. The participant chose crypto rather than the available gift card option.

Convert.

According to the transfer page, the points were redeemed as 0.00309135 BNB and sent to a wallet under the participant’s control.

The wallet provided a place to hold the asset. The transfer page did not show a date, and the rate and fee were not recorded.

Move.

On 3 September, 0.00315588 BNB was deposited into an exchange account. This was the only BNB deposit in the exchange history reviewed, covering 10 July to 21 October.

The deposit was approximately 2.1% larger than the amount shown on the redemption page. Without a dated transfer record linking the two, we cannot establish that they were the same funds.

On 5 September, the BNB was swapped to USDT, and 1.98 USDT from a source not recorded was added, giving a starting amount of 4.21 USDT. After two further swaps, 4.097171 USDT remained.

The difference was 0.112829 USDT, approximately 2.7%. This is the difference between the recorded amounts, not a measured transaction fee.

Price movement contributed to the difference; the individual fees were not separately recorded.

Out.

On 5 September, 4.097171 USDT left the exchange, with the withdrawal fee included in the recorded amount. The destination was not recorded.

A separate constraint applied to the intended M-PESA route: the reported minimum for P2P was $5. At approximately $4.10, this withdrawal was below that threshold and could not use that route under the reported conditions.

A separate test: June.

This was a different transaction, not a continuation of the route above.

In an earlier test, KSh 1,560 was used to buy BNB, which was converted to USDC. A total of 12.73981584 USDC was then sold for KSh 1,636.55 through M-PESA, at a reported rate of KSh 128.46 per USDC.

Part of the USDC came from another source. The June test demonstrates that an M-PESA conversion worked on that occasion. It does not establish where the September withdrawal went or what value was ultimately received from it.

What remains unrecorded

- The individual fees across the main transaction trail.

- The date of the wallet transfer.

- Whether the 3 September deposit represents the August redemption.

- The destination of the 5 September withdrawal.

- The source of the 1.98 USDT added on 5 September.

MODEL CHECK

The Good, the Bad, the Ugly

Good: Individual steps have supporting records.

Bad: The records do not establish an unbroken link between every step.

Ugly: A transaction can progress through several systems without revealing the final destination or the value ultimately received.

What this does not establish: the total cost of the route, the net value retained, whether the earning was worthwhile, or what happens to other participants.

SIGN-OFF

The Mantra

The reward appeared as points on a screen. The participant chose crypto over a gift card, then moved through a sequence of systems with different conditions.

The asset could be held and moved, but the available records do not establish one continuous route from the original redemption to the final destination.

A separate June test showed that conversion to M-PESA had worked before. It did not resolve the missing link in September.

In a transactional economy, each route has its own terms.

In the September test, the reported $5 P2P minimum placed a withdrawal of about $4.10 below that route’s threshold.

The infrastructure can be operational while the financial route remains constrained.

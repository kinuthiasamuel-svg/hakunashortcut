THE FRICTION POINT

The Unfiltered Realist

LOG 016 drew the Chain.

Access → Participation → Production → Ownership

LOG 017 took us to the crossroads and proposed nine conditions for reading what happens there.

Necessity. Opportunity. Constraint. Agency. Compliance. Capability. Shock. Information. Choice.

Until now, we had been drawing the model.

This log uses it.

The field introduces a simple problem.

-A digital message can travel in seconds.

A potato cannot.

-A buyer can send a request before the farmer has finished harvesting.

-A price can cross a network before a vehicle has reached the farm.

-A payment can settle in seconds, long after the physical work that earned it.

Between the signal and the sack is a space.

We had marked it as an open question.

This transmission walks into it.

THE BREAKDOWN

The Village Analyst

Method note.

The observations that follow come from two dated field notes in Nyandarua County, 11 and 12 August 2026, plus conversations. One note is from a farming area, the other from a neighbouring market town.

This is not a statistical sample.

They illustrate.

They do not prove.

The purpose is different.

Take the model into the field.

See where it holds.

See where it bends.

Record what it cannot explain.

The model would be wrong if conversion failures at the links showed no consistent pattern of conditions.

The working hypothesis

Information does not become action by itself.

Something has to convert it.

Productive capacity, what the surrounding system can execute, is the part of that conversion we can name so far.

There may be others.

Trust and Time keep appearing.

The first distinction

Digital capability and productive capacity are not the same thing.

Capability is what an actor can do.

A phone.

A skill.

A platform.

Know-how.

The ability to receive, interpret or transmit information.

Productive capacity is what the surrounding system can execute.

Labour.

Vehicles.

Roads.

Aggregation.

Grading.

Storage.

Working capital.

Coordination.

Institutional arrangements.

The distinction is simple

«I may be capable of doing something that my surrounding system cannot execute.»

And a second one, because it will be asked:

Constraint is the limit we hit.

Productive capacity is the system’s ability to relax it.

That is where the friction begins.

Where this layer comes from

The term productive capacities is an established concept; ://unctadstat.unctad.org/EN/Pci.html its application to the North Kinangop field test remains under investigation.

Our own archive first marked this layer in early September, as an open question at national scale:

Can an economy absorb, finance, manufacture, distribute and own what its people are able to build?

Here the question is smaller.

At the farm, what must exist for a message to become a delivered sack?

Same layer, or a different one at a different scale?

We do not know yet.

What we can say is this: it was missing from our map.

Not missing from the literature.

FIELD OBSERVATION

North Kinangop Dispatch

Potato chain: Production → Aggregation → Transport → Market & Payment

Four links.

One model.

Nine conditions.

Let’s walk it.

LINK 1 — PRODUCTION / HARVEST

A phone delivers the price.

That part is easy.

But a price signal does not harvest a potato.

Harvest timing matters.

Labour matters.

Road access matters.

Available volume matters.

The farmer may know exactly what the market is paying and still be unable to act on that information at that moment.

Dominant conditions observed: Constraint + Information.

The capability to interpret the price is present.

The capacity to execute the response is not.

First crack in the map.

LINK 2 — AGGREGATION

Digital communication can coordinate availability.

A message can identify farms.

A call can establish collection points.

A WhatsApp group can tell people who has produce.

But communication does not gather scattered potatoes into a marketable quantity.

Someone still has to assemble them.

On time.

At the right quality.

In the right place.

In sufficient volume.

And when every level of the chain uses its own scale, with no standard weight and no standard grade, every sale is also a negotiation over trust.

Dominant: Constraint + Agency.

Capacity gap: assembly, grading, a shared measure.

The network can coordinate the intention.

It cannot physically perform the aggregation.

LINK 3 — TRANSPORT

A buyer request can arrive instantly.

The commodity moves at the speed of available vehicles, roads and loading capacity.

This is where the distinction becomes impossible to ignore:

«Digital speed ≠ physical speed.»

For a perishable crop, the difference is not philosophical.

It has a price.

A vehicle that does not arrive.

A load that is not ready.

A quantity that cannot be assembled.

These are not failures of connectivity.

They are limits of conversion capacity.

Dominant: Constraint.

Capacity gap: vehicles, loading, road.

Shock appears only when timing is broken by something unexpected. A road washed out by rain. A vehicle that fails on the way.

Structural limitation is not the same thing as shock.

LINK 4 — MARKET & PAYMENT

The final signal may be the fastest of all.

Digital payment can settle quickly.

But payment does not create:

-bargaining power,

-guaranteed offtake,

-quality,

-transport,

-or the earlier links that made the transaction possible.

Two systems meet here.

Institutional buyers bring compliance, grading and delayed payment.

Folk buyers bring cash today, they buy everything, and they take no deductions.

For a farmer managing liquidity, the informal system often fits better.

What it does to the margin is a question this note cannot answer.

Dominant: Information + Agency + Constraint (market structure).

And here another question appears:

Who captures the value when the transaction succeeds?

Capability is not ownership.

Participation is not value capture.

Production is not automatically control.

WHAT THE FIELD SHOWED

Across the four links, several patterns repeat.

Digital tools reduce some friction.

They also redistribute friction.

Human judgement remains the active interface between information and action.

Informal networks continue alongside formal digital channels.

The physical system imposes limits that digital signals cannot override.

And most importantly:

«Information does not become action by itself.»

None of this declares digital technology ineffective.

It identifies the conditions under which its capability succeeds—

or stalls.

MODEL CHECK

The Good, the Bad, the Ugly

The good

Constraint appeared at every link.

Information moved fast.

Informal networks closed gaps that formal systems left open.

The bad

The model was too clean.

Capability and capacity had to be separated.

Shock needed a sharper edge.

Choice behaved like an outcome.

Constraint was everywhere, so it needs sub-types.

Trust and Time keep appearing, and neither is among the nine.

Necessity and Opportunity went untested here. Compliance appeared at Link 4, but was not examined. Absence of observation is not evidence of absence.

The ugly

An informal arrangement solved one bottleneck and left the others where they were.

A rational choice inside a weak system gave a poor result.

Value moved, and the people who generated it captured little of it.

And “productive capacity” could become a place to hide what we do not understand. If it explains every failure, it explains none.

The test did not invalidate the model.

It showed that its elements may not share the same logical status.

MODEL v1.1

LOG 017 proposed the model (v1.0).

LOG 018 revises it. This is a revision of logical status, not a validation.

Two terms that were too close now separate:

CAPABILITY

Agent-level.

What an actor can do.

PRODUCTIVE CAPACITY

System-level.

What the surrounding system can execute.

And two concepts move position:

CHOICE → the decision made. An observed response.

VALUE CAPTURE → observed outcome.

Agency stays what it was: the capacity to choose.

Open candidates: Trust. Time.

This is provisional, two dated field notes, 11–12 August 2026, plus conversations. One observer, limited terrain.

A second chain is the real test.
